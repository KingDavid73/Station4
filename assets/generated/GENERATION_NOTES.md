# Station 4 generated scene assets

Generated with the built-in image generation tool using the images in
`styleinspo/` as style, lighting, silhouette, and theatrical-composition
references. The references were not treated as edit targets.

## Shared art direction

Original low-poly theatrical diorama art with planar geometry, large dark
silhouettes, a straight-on audience viewpoint, charcoal/blue-gray/dirty-brown
color fields, and tiny warm amber or green practical lights. The room should
feel maintained and intimate rather than ruined, futuristic, or apocalyptic.

The master composition prompt specified:

- A locked 16:9 single-screen industrial back room.
- An old wooden desk on the far left.
- Three distinct 1960s/1970s mainframe monoliths across the rear wall.
- Pipes, conduit, and sagging wire bundles overhead.
- A broad open concrete walking floor in front.
- A parts graveyard on the far right, nearly swallowed by darkness.
- No character, UI, text, disaster effects, or active stage spotlights.

The isolated prop prompts reused the master image as their palette, scale, and
perspective anchor. They requested a flat magenta background, no cast shadow,
generous padding, and no magenta within the subject. The magenta was removed
locally with the image-generation skill's soft-matte and despill workflow.

## Character sheet prompt

The character is an original unnamed male technician in his late sixties:
slightly stooped, lean, short gray hair, muted olive-gray rolled-sleeve work
shirt, dark trousers, worn shoes, and a small brown tool belt. The sheet uses
the same low-poly theatrical silhouette language as the scene.

Rows:

1. Four-frame idle facing right.
2. Four-frame walk facing right.
3. Four-frame walk facing left.
4. Four-frame rear-view machine interaction.

## Runtime lighting note

The hover/select spotlight should be implemented as runtime lighting or a
masked light overlay, not baked into these images. That allows the same
spotlight to appear on hover, remain during walking and interaction, and fade
when the Technician returns to the desk.

## Modern station mirror set

The modern station was generated by using the old master composition as an
exact layout and camera anchor, then replacing its material and object
language while preserving the visual rhythm:

- Workstation remains at far left.
- Three tall computing cabinets remain across the rear wall.
- The broad walking floor remains open across the front.
- Replacement inventory occupies the same far-right region as the old parts
  graveyard.
- Enclosed service rails replace exposed pipes and sagging wire bundles.

The modern facility is bright, competent, sterile, sealed, and replaceable. It
is not intended to look sinister, luxurious, or science-fictional. Warm white,
pale gray, cool silver, and faint muted cyan replace the old room's charcoal,
dirty brown, olive, amber, and phosphor green.
